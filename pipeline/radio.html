Radio

Tune to a year between 1770 and 1810 and listen to what its newspapers printed, one morning after another. The radio starts on today's date in the year on the dial. Your browser reads aloud with its own voice; nothing is sent anywhere.

What you hear

  • The words are passages the newspapers printed on that date, as the archive transcribed them: one passage from each paper that day. Reviewed passages (1776 and 1797) come first; they were checked against the page scans. Matched passages were found automatically and not checked, so a machine misreading can be heard as it was transcribed.
  • The order: each day starts with a greeting to the town of the first paper. Then the radio moves to the next day with surviving pages. Days with no surviving page are skipped, which is a gap in the archive, not silence in the town.
  • The voice is your browser's own and differs between browsers and devices. Edge offers natural voices; Voice tries another.
  • The sounds are made in your browser for atmosphere: the hiss of the set, church bells, a ship's bell in harbour towns, horses on busy streets, birds in the country and a drum under war news. They are not recordings of the time. New York's police siren is a deliberate anachronism.
