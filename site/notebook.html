Notebook

How this project was made, told as it happened: what we wanted to know, what we decided and why, the wrong turns, the difficulties and how we got past them, and the care taken for the people who visit and for the people in the papers.

Each entry is dated, says what it changed, and links to the place on the site where that change lives. The quotes in gold are the maker's own words. Choose a kind to read one thread.

Beginnings · 27 October – 5 November

A dataset of 77,749 newspaper pages, a question about attention, and the decision to show what the papers printed rather than prove a large claim.

27 Oct
Purpose

The question we started with

Society has a limited amount of attention, and problems keep coming back. Does lasting change need sustained attention? The archive: American newspapers from 1770 to 1810, as the Smithsonian's Revolution Crossroads prepared them from the Library of Congress.

Still askedHow things come back, on Findings

The project's proposals of 1 November

early Nov
Decision

Show what was printed; let visitors judge

The share of a topic in the surviving papers is not the share of a society's attention, and the archive could not carry a proof of the big question. We chose an explorer of observed facts, “newspaper X printed this on date Y”, over a thesis.

Changedthe whole site: instruments, not a verdict

The research plan of 4 November

early Nov
DecisionCare

A count is a target, not a quota

Some story groups were thin, and the pulses would have looked richer with a few more printings in them. The rule: never pad a thin group to make the animation look better. What the papers did not print stays unprinted.

Building the instruments · 6 – 8 November

The pulses, the sky, and learning how far the machine's reading of the papers can be trusted.

6 Nov
FrustrationDifficulty overcome

The transcription left out what the paper printed

On 13 October 1797 the Gazette of the United States printed Captain Dunbar's full statement about a privateer; the archive's AI transcription simply dropped it, about 350 words. From then on, every reviewed printing was read against its page scan, and the misreadings are recorded beside the passages.

6 Nov
Decision

Trust the transcriptions to find stories, not to quote them

Reading every page by hand would take years. So the computer matched shared wording across all 41 years, 48,127 stories at first, kept apart from the stories checked by hand and labelled “not checked”.

Wrong turn

Colouring the stars by kind

We tried to colour stars as news, notices or other, from the topic model. A spot check found 2 or 3 of 14 “notices” right; a stricter cut still got 10 of 16. The colours would have told visitors something untrue, so they went.

For everyone who visits

An accessibility pass over every page: a link to skip the menu, names for every control, contrast, targets of at least 24 pixels, keyboard paths through the sliding controls, and pages that still read at 320 pixels wide, all checked with a real keyboard.

9 Nov
CareDecision

Names we did not print

In 1776, Philadelphia's committees printed notices naming people who refused the Continental bills, 18 printings. They name private people, singled out in their own day. We left them out of the reviewed stories: they could become a story of their own later, with the context they need.

Difficulty overcome

Twenty hours of recording, done by evening

At half the speed of speech, recording the titles of 7,031 stories would have taken this laptop about twenty hours. Split into two recordings running side by side, they were done the same evening, with no failures.

10 Nov
DiscoveryDifficulty overcome

A newspaper filed two years late

Reviewing 1808, the mastheads of the Kentucky Gazette said 1806. Every issue filed under 1806–1808 had been printed two years earlier: 106 issues. They were redated from their mastheads and matched again, and 4,506 new matches appeared.

DiscoveryDifficulty overcome

Pages filed under the wrong number

One misplaced page in 1807 led to a check of the whole dataset: 133 issues had their machine transcriptions filed under the wrong pages. 532 printings were moved back to the scans they belong to, so a reader who opens a page now sees the text that is on it.

10 Nov
CareDecision

Stories that wait for their context

Some reports were not admitted to the reviewed stories yet, among them the act prohibiting the importation of slaves, news from Saint-Domingue and Haiti, and reports on the Cherokee and Creek nations. They are not left out for good: they wait until they can be shown with the framing they need.

10 Nov
DecisionDifficulty overcome

A rule that fits the evidence

For 1793 only six to eight newspapers survive at a time, and only ten printed items reached the seven papers asked of other years. Rather than stretch the evidence or leave most of the year out, the threshold for 1793 became six, said openly.
