What you are looking at

One surviving archive

110 newspapers77,749 pages1770–1810

The Library of Congress’s scans of the newspapers that survive, gathered for the Smithsonian’s Revolution Crossroads, each page read by machine. It is what was kept, not everything that was printed.

Where the pages come from →

How much survives, and when

Only two newspapers survive for each of 1785 and 1786, against 10 to 17 a year after 1800. A thin year is a gap in the archive, not silence in the towns.

The finding →

Two kinds of evidence

PrintedſtavesRead by the machineslaves

48,270 stories were found by machine, by the wording their printings share; 98 were read and checked against the scans by hand, in 1776, 1793, 1797, 1807 and 1808. The machine’s reading changes a fact in about one passage in five, so the scans have the last word.
